Task: manage a full season of Harbin Heinong 58 soybean at standard density, focusing on the water risk at the R5/R6 pod-fill stage and on allocating limited irrigation water. Complete the full basic workflow: pre-planting, base fertilizer, planting, emergence, and early/mid-season checks of growth, nutrition, pests and disease. At R5/R6, use soil moisture, canopy/NDVI, thermal observation, growth stage and ground checks to decide whether there are high-priority ridge sections that are truly short of water. Constraints: Heinong 58 is stress-tolerant, so mild stress does not mean irrigating immediately; irrigation water is limited, so do not irrigate the whole field evenly. Success criteria: irrigate only the high-risk areas supported by field observations and state results, and before harvest confirm maturity, grain moisture, weather and field trafficability.
Known field and planting plan: 64 ridges, wide-ridge dense planting with two rows per ridge, ridge spacing 1.1 m, standard sowing depth 4.0 cm. ridges 0–63: HEINONG58, stress-tolerant variety at standard density, seed_spacing_cm=7.9 (plant spacing 7.9 cm), about 23.0 plants/m² (about 230,000 plants/ha).
Management focus: when water amount or the moisture window is limited, decide whether to irrigate specific areas based on soil moisture/VWC, canopy thermal anomalies, growth stage and variety stress tolerance.
Follow these steps:
1) Plant ridges 0-63: first confirm the seedbed, soil moisture, weather window, equipment and seed inventory; the purpose is to establish this scenario's variety, density and zoning.
2) Fertigation/top-up fertilizer for confirmed areas: first use soil/crop state to find abnormal leaf color, growth, NDVI or nutrition diagnosis signals, then use the drone to narrow down the abnormal area, and finally use the robot or a ground check to confirm that nutrition is the limiting factor rather than water, pests, disease or weeds; treat only the areas with a confirmed nutrition problem. The purpose is to restore nutrient-limited areas while saving fertigation resources.
3) Irrigate confirmed areas: first use soil moisture and crop state to find falling soil moisture/VWC or canopy thermal anomalies, then use the drone/thermal signal to locate the water-risk area, and finally use the robot or a ground check to confirm it is not a pest, disease, weed or nutrition problem; taking growth stage and the water budget into account, irrigate only the areas confirmed to be short of water and worth the water.
4) Harvest, then unload grain / dry if needed / store, for confirmed mature areas: confirm the area's maturity, grain moisture, weather and field trafficability; the purpose is to recover that area's yield in its window and store it safely.
Overall constraints: before every key treatment, first confirm the corresponding field evidence and resource status; do not treat the whole field uniformly just because a risk exists, and do not use one treatment to solve a different kind of stress. Harvest only when the crop is mature, grain moisture is known, the weather allows harvesting and the field is trafficable; batches at ≤13.5% moisture can be stored directly, wetter batches must first be dried to a safe moisture.

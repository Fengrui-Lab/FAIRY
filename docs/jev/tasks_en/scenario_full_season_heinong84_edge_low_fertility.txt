This is a Harbin full-season soybean scenario with a single standard variety. All 64 ridges are planted with Heinong 84 at standard density; spring, June, R5/R6 and the harvest period are normal overall. If after planting you find locally slow emergence, weak seedlings or uneven emergence, locate and diagnose the abnormal ridge sections through whole-field emergence checks, leaf color, NDVI, soil moisture, and signs of disease and pests. Complete pre-planting preparation, seed/base fertilizer, whole-field planting, emergence check, any needed local recovery treatment, early recovery recheck, nutrition check at early flowering as needed, mid-season scouting, R5/R6 water management, harvest at maturity, drying and safe storage.
Known field and planting plan: 64 ridges, wide-ridge dense planting with two rows per ridge, ridge spacing 1.1 m, standard sowing depth 4.0 cm. ridges 0–63: HEINONG84, standard density, seed_spacing_cm=7.9 (plant spacing 7.9 cm), about 23.0 plants/m² (about 230,000 plants/ha).
Management focus: decide whether to apply targeted fertilizer or fertigation based on leaf color/growth and nutrition diagnosis signals, leaf color/NDVI and ground checks.
Follow these steps:
1) Plant ridges 0-63: first confirm the seedbed, soil moisture, weather window, equipment and seed inventory; the purpose is to establish this scenario's variety, density and zoning.
2) Fertigation/top-up fertilizer for confirmed areas: first use soil/crop state to find abnormal leaf color, growth, NDVI or nutrition diagnosis signals, then use the drone to narrow down the abnormal area, and finally use the robot or a ground check to confirm that nutrition is the limiting factor rather than water, pests, disease or weeds; treat only the areas with a confirmed nutrition problem. The purpose is to restore nutrient-limited areas while saving fertigation resources.
3) Replant confirmed gap areas: based on emergence rate, stand gaps and the remaining season length, replant only the areas where it still pays off; the purpose is to restore the stand in gap areas and reduce early stand loss.

5) Harvest confirmed mature areas: confirm R8 maturity or that the crop meets harvest conditions, grain moisture, weather and field trafficability; the purpose is to recover yield and quality in the right window.
6) Unload grain, post-harvest batch: as soon as a harvest batch is done, form the post-harvest batch; the purpose is to keep the post-harvest steps in the correct order.
7) Harvest confirmed mature areas: confirm R8 maturity or that the crop meets harvest conditions, grain moisture, weather and field trafficability; the purpose is to recover yield and quality in the right window.
8) Unload grain, post-harvest batch: as soon as a harvest batch is done, form the post-harvest batch; the purpose is to keep the post-harvest steps in the correct order.
9) Dry the post-harvest batch: bring wetter batches down to a safe storage moisture first; the purpose is to ensure safe storage moisture.
10) Store the post-harvest batch: store it once the batch has reached safe moisture and storage capacity allows; the purpose is to complete a quality-safe storage loop.
Overall constraints: before every key treatment, first confirm the corresponding field evidence and resource status; do not treat the whole field uniformly just because a risk exists, and do not use one treatment to solve a different kind of stress. Harvest only when the crop is mature, grain moisture is known, the weather allows harvesting and the field is trafficable; batches at ≤13.5% moisture can be stored directly, wetter batches must first be dried to a safe moisture.

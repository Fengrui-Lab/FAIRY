Task: manage a full season of Harbin Heinong 84 soybean at standard density; when June is wet, focus on assessing local waterlogging, disease risk and the machinery work window. Complete the basic workflow: pre-planting, base fertilizer, planting, emergence, and early growth/nutrition checks; after the wet period, first use soil/canopy sensors to locate abnormal zones, then use the drone and ground robot to confirm disease, and combine weather, forecast and soil trafficability to decide whether spraying is possible. Constraints: do not spray the whole field immediately just because disease signs are seen; when the soil is too wet or the weather is unsuitable, wait for a suitable window; local treatment may only cover the abnormal ridge sections supported by field observations and state results. Success criteria: the disease diagnosis, work window, treatment area and harvest window can all be explained by earlier field observations and state results.
Known field and planting plan: 64 ridges, wide-ridge dense planting with two rows per ridge, ridge spacing 1.1 m, standard sowing depth 4.0 cm. ridges 0–63: HEINONG84, standard density, seed_spacing_cm=7.9 (plant spacing 7.9 cm), about 23.0 plants/m² (about 230,000 plants/ha).
Management focus: decide whether to apply targeted fungicide based on lesions and disease-confirmation signals, canopy/ground disease signs and the spraying window.
Follow these steps:
1) Plant ridges 0-63: first confirm the seedbed, soil moisture, weather window, equipment and seed inventory; the purpose is to establish this scenario's variety, density and zoning.
2) Apply fungicide to confirmed areas: first use sensor/canopy state to find suspected anomalies, then use the drone to locate canopy or NDVI anomaly areas, and finally use the robot or a ground check to confirm lesions, disease-confirmation signals and the spraying weather; apply fungicide to confirmed disease areas only when there is enough evidence for treatment.
3) Fertigation/top-up fertilizer for confirmed areas: first use soil/crop state to find abnormal leaf color, growth, NDVI or nutrition diagnosis signals, then use the drone to narrow down the abnormal area, and finally use the robot or a ground check to confirm that nutrition is the limiting factor rather than water, pests, disease or weeds; treat only the areas with a confirmed nutrition problem. The purpose is to restore nutrient-limited areas while saving fertigation resources.
4) Harvest, then unload grain / dry if needed / store, for confirmed mature areas: confirm the area's maturity, grain moisture, weather and field trafficability; the purpose is to recover that area's yield in its window and store it safely.
Overall constraints: before every key treatment, first confirm the corresponding field evidence and resource status; do not treat the whole field uniformly just because a risk exists, and do not use one treatment to solve a different kind of stress. Harvest only when the crop is mature, grain moisture is known, the weather allows harvesting and the field is trafficable; batches at ≤13.5% moisture can be stored directly, wetter batches must first be dried to a safe moisture.

Task: manage a local leaf-color anomaly at early flowering. First confirm that water and pests/disease are normal, then decide whether a small dose of micronutrient/foliar nutrition is needed; do not spray pesticide or irrigate by mistake.
Known field and planting plan: 64 ridges, wide-ridge dense planting with two rows per ridge, ridge spacing 1.1 m, standard sowing depth 4.0 cm. ridges 0–63: HEINONG84, standard density, seed_spacing_cm=7.9 (plant spacing 7.9 cm), about 23.0 plants/m² (about 230,000 plants/ha).
Management focus: decide whether to apply targeted fertilizer or fertigation based on leaf color/growth and nutrition diagnosis signals, leaf color/NDVI and ground checks.
Follow these steps:
1) Plant ridges 0-63: first confirm the seedbed, soil moisture, weather window, equipment and seed inventory; the purpose is to establish this scenario's variety, density and zoning.
2) Fertigation/top-up fertilizer for confirmed areas: first use soil/crop state to find abnormal leaf color, growth, NDVI or nutrition diagnosis signals, then use the drone to narrow down the abnormal area, and finally use the robot or a ground check to confirm that nutrition is the limiting factor rather than water, pests, disease or weeds; treat only the areas with a confirmed nutrition problem. The purpose is to restore nutrient-limited areas while saving fertigation resources.
3) Harvest, then unload grain / dry if needed / store, for confirmed mature areas: confirm the area's maturity, grain moisture, weather and field trafficability; the purpose is to recover that area's yield in its window and store it safely.
Overall constraints: before every key treatment, first confirm the corresponding field evidence and resource status; do not treat the whole field uniformly just because a risk exists, and do not use one treatment to solve a different kind of stress. Harvest only when the crop is mature, grain moisture is known, the weather allows harvesting and the field is trafficable; batches at ≤13.5% moisture can be stored directly, wetter batches must first be dried to a safe moisture.
